# The Primed Nidra Protocol

**Autogenic training and yoga nidra, each repairing what the other lacks.**

---

## Thesis

These two practices fail in opposite directions, and each one's failure is the
other's strength. That is the whole argument; everything below is its
consequence.

| | Yoga nidra | Autogenic training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Induction** | Long, and delivered *to* you | Ninety seconds, self-issued |
| **Content once you are in** | Rich — opposites, witness, sankalpa | Almost none |
| **How it is taught** | A voice, indefinitely | One element at a time, invariant wording, three short reps a day |
| **When you progress** | Whenever the teacher says | When a criterion is met |
| **Where you can do it** | Lying down, undisturbed, headphones | A car park, a train, a desk |
| **If you lose consciousness** | The voice carries on without you | The practice stops — it needs you to issue it |
| **How it ends** | When the recording ends | You choose: cancel and be alert, or omit and sleep |

**What AT gives nidra.** Not a faster induction — a *training method*, and an
answer to the sleep problem below. AT is the
only relaxation tradition that treats consolidation as an engineering problem:
fix the wording, drill one element at a time, three short reps a day, and let a
competence criterion rather than a calendar decide when you move on. Nidra has
no equivalent, which is why practitioners of twenty years still need a voice to
walk them through it. That, and portability, and an exit you control.

### Length is not a minor complaint — it is the mechanism of the main failure

The commonest thing that happens in a yoga nidra session is that the
practitioner falls asleep, and length is why. Thirty-five minutes, supine,
warm, eyes closed, a soft voice, and almost nothing asked of you moment to
moment: those are not merely conditions under which sleep is possible, they are
the standard clinical recipe for inducing it. The practice is aiming at the
threshold — conscious, on the edge — and holds you there for long enough that
sleep pressure reliably wins.

**The recording is what allows it.** A voice continues whether or not anyone is
listening, so nothing in the system notices you have gone. That is the deep
structural flaw, and it is not fixable by making a better recording. A
self-issued sequence has the check built in for free: if you fall asleep during
autogenic training, *the practice stops*, because it will not proceed without
you issuing the next formula. Nobody has to notice — the absence of the next
step is the noticing.

This is also why "just do a shorter nidra" is not the answer. Without a
self-issued induction, a shorter nidra is only a rushed one; the length is
carrying the depth. Replacing the long entry with a fast one you perform
yourself is what makes the shortening possible rather than merely faster.

**Length costs a second thing, which is easy to miss:** it costs the dose.
Thirty-five minutes is a practice you do weekly. Ninety seconds is a practice
you do three times a day. Autogenic training's central claim about frequency —
that short and often builds the response and long and rare does not — makes
that an argument about efficacy, not convenience. A practice too long to repeat
is under-dosed by construction, however good each session is.

And sleep is not always the failure. It is the failure only when it was not
chosen, which is precisely what nidra gives you no control over. See the
cancellation, below.

**What nidra gives AT.** Content. AT trains deliberate access to a state and
then, for most practitioners, stops — the six standard exercises are *all*
induction. What lies beyond them (Schultz's meditative exercises, Luthe's
intentional formulae and autogenic modification) is real but sparsely
documented in English, almost always instructor-taught, and rarely practised.
So the common experience is a well-trained switch with nothing on the other
side of it. Nidra has spent centuries on exactly that: what to *do* in the
state. Pairs of opposites, witness, the sowing of a resolve.

The pairing is symmetric. AT supplies the method and the door; nidra supplies
the room.

### The bet, stated plainly

This protocol replaces nidra's rotation of consciousness with an autogenic
induction. That is a **bet**, not an established equivalence, and it should be
held as one.

The optimistic reading: rotation is transport — a systematic way of arriving,
which AT achieves faster and without a voice.

The pessimistic reading, which is not obviously wrong: **rotation is the
practice.** A structured attention drill in its own right, not scaffolding, in
which case an autogenic pass gets you somewhere adjacent rather than the same
place.

The honest position is that this is untested and the protocol is how you test
it. If after twelve weeks the content stages land as well from an autogenic
entry as from a rotation entry, the bet paid. If they feel thinner, rotation
was doing work, and the right answer is a hybrid entry rather than a
substitution. **Week 10 is where you find out** — see the review point.

### The central device: the cancellation is the switch

Same practice, two outcomes. Perform the AT cancellation at the end and you are
alert, restored, back to work. Omit it and the practice runs downhill into
sleep. This is the one control a recording structurally cannot give you,
because a recording does not know which you need tonight.

Read against the sleep problem above, this is the actual repair. Nidra's
trouble is not that people fall asleep — it is that falling asleep is not a
*decision*. The same session drifts into sleep at 10pm, which is fine, and at
2pm, which wastes the afternoon, and the practitioner has no lever either way.
Making the exit an explicit act you perform turns an unreliable outcome into a
switch with two labelled positions.

### On the sankalpa's position

Classical nidra sows the sankalpa *after* initial relaxation, not at the
threshold — the mind has to be receptive for sowing to be anything more than
recitation. The first statement therefore sits immediately after the autogenic
pass, which is the compressed equivalent of that classical slot, and repeats
verbatim before externalisation. The entry carries no content beyond the
opening formula: the ramp does one job.

## Phase 0 — The integrated ladder (13 weeks)

The obvious mistake is to learn AT first and bolt nidra on afterwards. That
wastes the pedagogy, which is the thing worth borrowing. Instead each nidra
element is grafted onto the AT formula sharing its substrate, and trained by
the same method.

**Session structure:** three sessions daily, 3–5 minutes each. Two of the three
carry a 40-second nidra graft, appended after the AT formula and before the
cancellation. The third stays pure AT.

| Wk | AT formula | Grafted nidra drill (40s) | Criterion to progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *My right arm is heavy* | — none — | Heaviness <30s, unforced |
| 2 | Heaviness → both arms | — none — | Both arms, unforced |
| 3 | Heaviness → legs; *arms and legs are heavy* | **Opposites 1** — heavy / light | Hold both poles 10s |
| 4 | *My right arm is warm* → generalise | **Refuge** — enter via warmth and contact only | Arrives <10s as sensation |
| 5 | *My heartbeat is calm and regular* | **Opposites 2** — cool / warm | Hold both 10s |
| 6 | *It breathes me* | **Pranamaya** — count breaths 11→1 | Reach 1 with the count *recovered* after a lapse, not unbroken |
| 7 | *My solar plexus is warm* | **Opposites 3** — tense / at ease | Hold both 10s |
| 8 | *My forehead is cool* | **Sakshi**, 60s | *No criterion — time only* |
| 9 | Generalisation formula ×1 | **Sankalpa — enter Question mode** | *No criterion — runs until it resolves* |
| 10 | Generalisation ×2 | **Opposites 4** — unwelcome / welcome | Hold both 10s |
| 11 | Generalisation ×2 | **Opposites 5** — sadness / joy | Hold both 10s |
| 12 | Assembly — full 12-min, card read *beforehand* | — | Complete without consulting card |
| 13 | Full 12-min, silent, timer chimes only | — | — |

Three deliberate choices in that table:

**Weeks 1–2 carry no graft.** The first formula is the foundation for
everything downstream and should not compete for attention. Generalising
heaviness from one arm to both is itself a step; adding a nidra drill in the
same week would make it the heaviest week in the ladder, immediately after the
lightest, in a protocol whose whole principle is one element at a time.

**Legs get their own week.** Heaviness in autogenic training is a *limb*
response and the consolidated formula names both — *my arms and legs are
heavy*. Dropping the legs, as short courses often do, teaches three quarters of
the first standard exercise.

**Affective pairs are held to weeks 10–11**, because they are felt in the gut
and the solar plexus formula that trains that substrate is not in place until
week 7.

The sankalpa slot is *occupied* from week 1 in simplify-being mode, even though
nothing is trained there until week 9. The sequence needs to learn its own
shape early; retrofitting a stage into an already-automatic chain is far harder
than leaving a placeholder in it.

**Opening formula** (every session): *I am completely calm.*

**Cancellation** (every session, always): clench and flex both arms vigorously
×3 → one deep breath → open eyes. Deliberately brisk. Not decoration — it is
what prevents grogginess and the unintended drift into sleep.

**The generalisation formula** — the finished induction, one pass, 60–90s:

> *My arms and legs are heavy and warm. My heartbeat and breathing are calm and
> regular. My solar plexus is warm. My forehead is cool. I am completely calm.*

### When it goes wrong

Thirteen weeks at three sessions a day is a serious ask, and the realistic
failure is not a missed day. It is stopping in week 3 and finding the whole
thing too daunting to resume. So:

- **Under two weeks away:** resume at the week you left, no penalty. The
  criterion will tell you if you have actually lost ground.
- **Two weeks to two months:** drop back two weeks and re-run them. Faster than
  it sounds — recovery is much quicker than acquisition.
- **Longer than that:** restart at week 1, and expect to move through the early
  weeks in days rather than weeks. The criteria are the same; you will just
  meet them sooner.
- **Repeatedly stalling at the same week:** the graft is too much, not the AT.
  Run that week's AT formula alone until its criterion is met, then add the
  graft on its own for a week. Splitting a week in two is always allowed.

**The ladder is a floor, not a target.** Progression is gated by the criterion,
never by the week number. Three short sessions beat one long one, every time.

## Choosing the sankalpa — three modes

This is the most common place people stall, because the practice appears to
demand a settled life-resolve before you are allowed to begin, and arriving at
one is the work of months. The invariance rule below makes premature choice
expensive. So the slot runs in one of three modes and never blocks you.

The three are named for what they are rather than after anyone's framework —
the terms here were borrowed from a modern author's vocabulary and have been
replaced with plain description.

**Mode 1 — Presence.** The slot is held open with no content. Arrival, nothing
asked for. Available from session one, requires no decision, and is a complete
practice in its own right rather than a placeholder to be embarrassed about.

**Mode 2 — Question.** Rather than stating a resolve, hold one and listen.
*What do I most deeply want?* Ask once, listen roughly twenty seconds, and if
nothing arrives, drop it entirely and move on.

**Mode 3 — Resolve.** The classical form: a short fixed statement, present
tense, phrased as already true, felt in the body rather than thought.
Invariance applies here and only here.

### The modes are a pipeline, not a difficulty gradient

Mode 2 is not an easier substitute for mode 3 — it is the mechanism that
*produces* mode 3. Nobody selects a sankalpa from a menu; it surfaces
repeatedly until you notice it has been the same one for a month. Which gives
the transition a real criterion:

> **Move from Question to Resolve when the same answer has arrived unbidden
> across roughly five consecutive sessions, and lands as a felt yes rather than
> as a good idea.**

Still generating fresh candidates each session means not ready. That is
information, not failure — and the negative result is more useful than a
resolve adopted on schedule.

**Question's failure mode is thinking.** Problem-solving your life while
lying down is the exact opposite of the state the induction just produced. The
twenty-second cap is what prevents it. Answers arrive between sessions at least
as often as during them.

**The two slots can run at different modes.** The 2:00 sowing carries the
long-term sankalpa; the 10:00 slot carries the current chapter's Intention,
which by design turns over. A settled practitioner will often run mode 3 at
2:00 and mode 2 at 10:00 indefinitely.

## Making the nidra elements automatic

Three mechanisms, all borrowed from AT and applied to material AT never covered.

**1. Invariant wording.** AT's phrases never change, and that invariance is
precisely what lets a sentence stop being an instruction and become a cue.
Nidra stages fail to consolidate largely because every teacher and every
recording paraphrases them differently. Fix the wording of every drill once, in
writing, and never improve it. The urge to improve the phrasing is the enemy of
automaticity. For the sankalpa this applies in mode 3 only — locking wording
you have not arrived at yet is what creates the paralysis described above.

**2. Cue-shrinking.** Each drill starts as a full internal instruction and
compresses as it consolidates:

| Stage | Opposites drill, as issued internally |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 of a pair | *"Let heaviness fill the arms. Now release it and find lightness. Now both at once."* |
| Weeks 3–4 | *"Heavy… light… both."* |
| Mature | *"Both."* |

The endpoint is a single token that unpacks the whole operation. That is what
automatic means here — not that you do it without thinking, but that the
instruction has collapsed to a size that costs nothing to issue.

**3. The sankalpa is already an autogenic formula.** Luthe's advanced work
includes *intentional formulae* — short, present-tense, invariant
self-statements practised after the standard exercises. Structurally that is
the sankalpa. So it needs no separate training method: train it as the seventh
formula, same schedule, same rule that the wording is fixed for good. *(Cite
the volume rather than this paragraph — the concept is well attested in Luthe's
Autogenic Therapy series, but the exact terminology is worth quoting from the
source if this goes anywhere public.)*

### Weaning off the recording

Consolidation fails if you keep listening, because recognition never becomes
retrieval. One step at a time, about a week each:

1. Full guided track, as normal.
2. Guided, but skip the induction — start at the rotation of consciousness.
3. Guided, and silently issue each stage cue **before the voice does**. *This is
   the load-bearing step*, for two reasons. Anticipating the instruction forces
   retrieval, where listening to it only rehearses recognition — and it is the
   first step in the ladder you cannot sleep through, because staying ahead of
   the voice is a continuous demand. If you drift, you notice immediately: the
   voice says something you had not yet issued.
4. Silent, with a timer chiming stage boundaries.
5. Silent, no timer.

**Card discipline:** read the stage sequence for ten seconds with eyes open
*before* lying down, then run the session blind. Consulting the card
mid-practice re-externalises the scaffold you are trying to remove, and one
glance undoes a week of step 3.

### What not to automate

Sakshi and anandamaya get time, not reps, and never a criterion. They are
defined by the absence of a doing, so drilling them toward automaticity is a
category error — you would be conditioning a manoeuvre where the point is that
no manoeuvre occurs. They become reliable as a *consequence* of the other
stages consolidating and freeing up attention, not as a trained skill.

## Phase 1 — The 12-minute primed nidra

| Time | Stage | What you do | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **Settle** | Supine, eyes closed. Opening formula only — no content, no resolve. | AT |
| 0:30–2:00 | **Induction** | Generalisation formula ×2, unhurried. Stands in for the rotation of consciousness. | AT |
| 2:00–2:30 | **Sankalpa (sowing)** | Whichever mode is current — Presence, Question, or the fixed Resolve stated as already true. | Nidra |
| 2:30–3:30 | **Refuge** | Go to it as *sensation*, not as a picture. Warmth, contact, weight. Establish it is available, then leave it. | Nidra |
| 3:30–5:00 | **Pranamaya** | *It breathes me.* Then count breaths down from 11, restarting on any lapse without annoyance. | Both |
| 5:00–8:00 | **Pairs of opposites** | One pair per session, ~45s each pole, then both simultaneously — the simultaneity is the actual work. | Nidra |
| 8:00–10:00 | **Sakshi / anandamaya** | Drop the object. Rest as the awareness present through every stage, including the AT sensations. No formula, no task. | Nidra |
| 10:00–11:30 | **Sankalpa (repeat) + Intention** | Sankalpa again, verbatim if in Resolve mode. Then the Intention, often Question mode indefinitely. Let both land in the softened state rather than being asserted at it. | Nidra |
| 11:30–12:00 | **Externalisation** | AT cancellation, brisk. Sit up before the eyes fully re-engage. | Both |

The closing slot runs 90 seconds, not 60: a verbatim sankalpa followed by an
enquiry that needs its own twenty seconds of listening does not fit in a
minute. The cancellation takes fifteen seconds and does not need sixty.

### The opposites ladder — cycle one per session

1. Heavy / light *(easiest — piggybacks on the AT heaviness formula)*
2. Cool / warm *(same trick, and forehead-cool has trained one half)*
3. Tense / at ease
4. Effortful / effortless
5. Restless / settled
6. Unwelcome / welcome
7. Sadness / joy
8. Fear / safety *(use the Refuge as the anchor)*
9. Small self / spacious

Pairs 1–3 are where the two traditions genuinely fuse. You already have
voluntary control of heaviness and warmth, so the opposites practice begins on
a sensation you can *produce*, not merely observe. Learning dvandva cold gives
you nothing to produce. This is the strongest single argument for the hybrid,
and the first place to look for evidence that the bet paid.

## Phase 2 — The variants

**Field version — 3 minutes.** Car park before a customer meeting; seated, eyes
closed or soft-gaze down.

> Generalisation formula ×1 → Refuge, 30s → sankalpa, once (mode 1 is fine) →
> cancellation.

No opposites, no sakshi. This is the version that justifies the whole Phase 0
investment: yoga nidra has no equivalent and never will, because you cannot lie
down with headphones in a client car park.

**Sleep version — in bed, no clock.**

> Generalisation formula, slowed to half pace → rotation of consciousness, right
> side to left, unhurried → **no opposites, no sakshi, no cancellation.**

Deliberately unfinished. Removing the cancellation removes the exit, and the
practice runs down into sleep — exactly what you want here and exactly what you
do not want at 2pm on a Tuesday.

*On the sankalpa here:* the classical argument is that hypnagogia is precisely
*why* a resolve is sown near sleep, and dropping it overrules the tradition
rather than applying it. Try it both ways. The case for dropping it is that a
resolve you sleep through is unrehearsed; the case against is that the
receptive state is the whole point. This is genuinely open.

**Long version — keep the recordings.** On days you have 35 minutes, run a full
guided track properly. The recordings are how you *learn* the content stages;
this protocol is how you *deploy* them. Retiring them too early leaves you with
a fast induction and nothing to induce toward.

## Measuring it

**AT competence — the one that matters most.** Time-to-warmth in the dominant
hand, timed weekly with a stopwatch. Expect it to fall substantially across the
ladder; a plateau, or a rise, usually means striving rather than a lack of
practice, and the response is to back off the effort, not the frequency.

*No published norms exist for this.* Any specific target — "under 20 seconds by
week 8" — would be invention. Record your own numbers from week 3 and treat the
trend as the signal. Your week-3 figure is your baseline, not a benchmark.

**Autonomic response.** If you have a chest strap: RMSSD during the session
against a matched 12-minute seated baseline, plus a reading ten minutes after.
The post-session number is the more useful one — carryover is what you are
actually training.

Keep the claim modest. The AT-and-HRV literature is small — a handful of
studies, plus an increase in the high-frequency component reported in Seo &
Kim's 2019 meta-analysis — not a consistently replicated finding, and RMSSD and
HF-HRV are related but not interchangeable measures. Treat any movement as a
personal signal, not a demonstration.

**Subjective.** Activation 0–10, before and after, one number each. Not a
journal. Anything more elaborate becomes a task, and the task becomes the
reason you skip the practice.

### Review point — week 11

The question is not *do I feel calmer.* It is: **can I reach the state in 90
seconds, in a non-ideal environment?**

**If yes:** the hybrid has done its job. Move to Phase 1 proper and start
alternating with full recordings on long days.

**If no** — and this is the branch the first draft of this document did not
have:

- *Reaching the state takes longer than 90s but does happen.* Not a failure.
  Stay in Phase 0, keep the frequency, drop the grafts for two weeks and let
  the AT consolidate alone. The grafts are competing for attention.
- *It works lying down but not seated in a car park.* The induction is fine;
  the generalisation is not. Practise the field version specifically, seated,
  as one of your three daily sessions.
- *I keep falling asleep in the 12-minute session.* Expected, and it is the
  failure this protocol is built to fix, so treat it as a signal rather than a
  verdict. In order: practise seated rather than supine for a fortnight; move
  the session away from the end of the day; and check you are actually
  performing the cancellation every time, since skipping it teaches the
  sequence to end in sleep. If it persists while the field version stays
  reliable, you are not under-trained — you are sleep-deprived, and the
  practice is reporting it accurately.
- *It does not arrive at all, and has not for weeks.* Check the four usual
  causes in order: trying too hard, sessions too long, too few per day, a
  posture you cannot relax in. In that order, and one at a time.
- *The content stages feel thinner than they do from a recording.* This is the
  bet failing, and it is worth knowing. The answer is not to abandon the
  hybrid but to lengthen the entry — restore a short rotation of consciousness
  after the autogenic pass and see whether the stages recover. If they do,
  rotation was doing work, and the honest version of this protocol is AT
  *plus* an abbreviated rotation rather than AT *instead of* it.

## Cautions

- **Autogenic discharges** are normal and expected during Phase 0: muscle
  twitches, transient tearfulness, brief dizziness, sensations of floating or
  asymmetry. They usually indicate the practice is working. Shorten the session
  and always perform the cancellation. If a discharge is strongly emotional and
  persists across sessions, that is the point to work with a trained AT
  practitioner rather than push through alone.
- **The heart and solar plexus formulas** are the two traditionally taught
  under supervision. With any cardiac history, blood-pressure medication, or
  blood-sugar management in play, get a GP's view before adding weeks 5 and 7.
- **Not while driving.** Including the field version. Obvious, and yet.
- **This is a self-training method, not medical treatment or advice.**

## Naming note (if this becomes a product)

The Sanskrit stage names — sankalpa, nyasa, pranamaya, dvandva, sakshi,
anandamaya — are traditional and unencumbered. "Yoga nidra" itself is a generic
practice name. Two things to keep clear of: proprietary system names belonging
to specific modern lineages, and the published scripts of any particular
school, which are copyrighted even where the stage structure is not. Any
recorded guidance needs writing from scratch.

## What already exists

The left-hand column of Phase 0 — the AT ladder — is built and testable: see
this repository. Ten steps, one formula at a time, invariant wording, and a
gate that requires seven days *plus* evidence the response is arriving unbidden
before it opens the next step. That gate is the "criterion rather than a
calendar" this document argues nidra lacks, already implemented.

Phase 1 and the two variants are now built as well, under Settings → Primed
nidra, marked experimental. What is there is a **silent staged timer**, and the
silence is the point: every nidra tool on the market is a recording, and a
recording is the one thing this protocol removes. It chimes at each stage
boundary, shows a cue rather than narration, and never speaks.

**The induction is whatever formulae you have.** It was the full generalisation
formula, which meant nidra could not start until the whole ladder was finished
— a rule with nothing behind it, since the induction's only job is to produce
the state, and heaviness and warmth produce it for most people. It is now built
from the sequence you are actually practising, so it grows as you do.

That moves the first real session a long way forward. The three-minute version
is settle, induction, refuge, sankalpa and the cancellation, so refuge is the
only content element it needs — and refuge is grafted onto warmth. Anyone
holding heaviness and warmth has everything that session requires. Locked
lengths stay on screen and name what opens them, because the path is more use
than a hidden button.

Three lengths, matching the document:

| | Stages | Runs for | Opens after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 min | Settle, Induction, Refuge, Sankalpa, Cancellation | 180s | Refuge |
| 7 min | Settle, Induction, Sankalpa, Refuge, Pranamaya, Opposites, Cancellation | 420s | Pranamaya |
| 12 min | Settle, Induction, Sankalpa, Refuge, Pranamaya, Opposites, Sakshi, Sankalpa again, Cancellation | 720s, tiled exactly | Sakshi |
| Sleep | Induction, Rotation, No exit | open-ended, counting up | Refuge |

Each length opens when the drills it is made of have been trained, which is why
there are three of them rather than two. The drills arrive in order — refuge,
opposites, counting the breath — so by the breathing step a seven-minute
session is genuinely available, and without it there was nothing at all between
three minutes and the end of the ladder, across the stretch with least to look
forward to. Sleep is not a rung: it shares its prerequisite with the short
session, so it is a variant on the card rather than a level of its own.

The sankalpa can be written down, and in Resolve mode it is what appears at
both sankalpa stages — in the words it was written in and no others. Rewording
it after it has been running asks for confirmation, because the same sentence
every time is the discipline autogenic training lends nidra, and a resolve
edited every fortnight is a wish list the practice cannot get behind. After
twenty sessions the app asks once whether it is still the one.

Cue-shrinking is implemented as the document specifies it, keyed to completed
sessions: full wording under 8, compressed under 20, one word after that. The
opposites pair advances one step per completed session, so the nine pairs cycle
rather than being chosen. Sankalpa mode switches the wording of both sankalpa
stages between the three modes above. The sleep variant has no arc, no
countdown and no cancellation stage, and does not count towards shrinking —
stopping it in the morning is not a completed session.

The 40-second drills are built, as grafts rather than as a separate practice.
Each is appended to the formula that shares its substrate — refuge onto warmth,
counting breaths onto the breath — and carried by two of the day's three
sessions, with the review left as pure autogenic training. That is this
document's own ratio. Guided sessions never carry one, because the recordings
have no room for it and the graft is self-issued anyway.

The ladder is shorter than the table above, so the grafts moved with it: none
on the first two steps, then refuge, opposites, pranamaya, opposites, sakshi
and the sankalpa question. Wording is fixed and shrinks on days carried, by the
same mechanism as the formulae.

Criteria are stated and not gated. The ladder already paces progression, and a
second gate on a self-reported ten seconds would be a scaffold pretending to be
a ruler. Sakshi and the sankalpa show *no criterion* at all, as this document
requires: they get time, not repetitions.

Still unbuilt: the weaning ladder — though most of it is moot here, since the
runner has been silent from the start. Steps 1 to 3 assume a recording to wean
off; what remains is 4 and 5, silent with chimes and then silent without, and
the app only does the first.
