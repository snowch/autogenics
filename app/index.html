The ladder

The whole method

Heaviness is the first of six standard exercises. Each is learned the same way and added to the one before, so a full practice runs through all of them in about the same ninety seconds.

And after the six

The standard exercises are the foundation, not the whole of it. Beyond them sit the meditative exercises — sustained attention on colour, on objects, on abstract ideas and feeling states — and personal phrases of your own, aimed at a specific habit or intention, which only work once the six are solid.

The later standard exercises, particularly the heart and abdominal ones, are traditionally taught with a trained instructor rather than from a recording. This app covers heaviness. The rest is mapped here so you can see where it goes.

Key ideas

You cannot make it happen

Effort is the one thing that reliably blocks it. Hold the phrase lightly, notice whatever is actually there, and let it be uninteresting. It is like falling asleep: you can arrange the conditions, but you can't force the moment.

Say it in your own voice

The phrase is yours, thought silently, not listened to. The recordings only show you the shape — the practice is you thinking the words, in your head, at your own pace. In the older books these phrases are called formulae.

Short and often, not long and rare

Ninety seconds, three times a day. Frequency builds the response; duration does not. One long session a day is not the same thing and will not do the same thing.

Nothing happening is normal

Expect nothing for the first few days. Heaviness usually appears somewhere between a few days and two weeks. It may arrive as warmth, tingling, sinking, or the arm feeling larger or further away — those all count.

If restlessness or emotion comes up

That is a recognised part of the process — the old books call it an autogenic discharge — and not a sign that something is wrong. If it is uncomfortable: open your eyes, move your arms and legs, and stop there for that session.

New to the method?

Autogenic training was worked out in Berlin in the 1920s and taught ever since. You repeat a short phrase to yourself — my right arm is heavy — and let it be true rather than making it true. Do that often enough and the shift becomes something you can reach for deliberately, which is the whole point: not to relax now, but to be able to.

Why people practise it

After a couple of weeks most people can drop into it in ninety seconds. The arms go heavy and warm, the shoulders stop holding on, the body quietly stops bracing.

People reach for it at ordinary moments: winding down after a rough day, settling into sleep, steadying themselves before something that matters. Because it takes ninety seconds and no equipment, you can use it when you need it rather than once the moment has passed.

Why it's a skill, not a session

It's a physical skill, not a belief system — you're practising something your body already knows how to do. That's why this app steps back as you improve: the point is to end up not needing it.

Why bother

Ninety seconds, three times a day, and in about a fortnight you can produce the state without any audio at all — at a desk, on a train, before sleep. It is a skill you keep rather than an app you keep opening.

What it actually feels like

Warmth and weight in the arms, first faintly and then plainly. Most people notice the shoulders letting go before anything else. It is undramatic on purpose — the aim is a state you can reach without ceremony, not an experience.

This is a self-training method, not medical treatment or advice. If something is wrong, see a doctor.

Before you practise

Never while driving, cycling, or doing anything that needs your attention. Deep relaxation can leave you light-headed when you get up — if you have low blood pressure, a heart condition, diabetes, or epilepsy, speak to your doctor before making it a daily habit.

Always finish by waking yourself up: bend and stretch your arms firmly, fists clenched; two deep breaths; open your eyes. It takes about fifteen seconds and it is what stops you feeling groggy afterwards. The one exception is practising in bed to fall asleep — then stay lying down. (Traditionally this is called the take-back.)

Before you start

What this is, and what it isn't.

Autogenic training is a self-training method: you learn deliberate access to a state your body normally only reaches on its own. This app teaches it and keeps a record of your practice.

It is not medical treatment and nothing here is medical advice. It does not diagnose or treat any condition. If something is wrong, see a doctor — this sits alongside that, never instead of it.

Every practice ends by waking you up again — arms, breath, eyes — and the app always runs that part. Two of the later exercises are ones to learn in person rather than from a recording, and it will tell you when you reach them.

What people notice

An off switch you can reach for.

  • 01Less noise in your headNot silence — the volume down. Most people notice the mental chatter thinning out before they notice anything in their arms.
  • 02Coming down fasterInstead of staying wound up for hours after something stressful, you have something to do about it that takes ninety seconds.
  • 03Settling into sleepThe most common reason people keep it up. It is what the practice is doing anyway, aimed at bedtime.
  • 04A cleaner head afterwardsPlenty of people use it as a reset between two demanding things, rather than to relax at the end of the day.
  • 05Steadier beforehandBefore the interview, the talk, the difficult conversation — something to do with your hands and your attention that isn't rehearsing.

What arrives, and when, varies a lot between people. None of this is promised, and none of it is a medical outcome.
