      Before you practise

      • Never while driving or cycling. Or anything else that needs your attention.
      • Always wake yourself up afterwards. The app runs that part — about fifteen seconds.
      Check with your doctor first if…

      Deep relaxation can leave you light-headed when you get up. If you have low blood pressure, a heart condition, diabetes, or epilepsy, speak to your doctor before making this a daily habit.

      How to wake yourself up

      Bend and stretch your arms firmly, fists clenched; two deep breaths; open your eyes. About fifteen seconds, and it is what stops you feeling groggy. The one exception is practising in bed to fall asleep — then stay lying down. (Traditionally this is called the take-back.)

      A self-training method, not medical treatment or advice. It does not diagnose or treat any condition. If something is wrong, see a doctor.

      Doing it right

      You cannot make it happen

      Let the phrase be true rather than making it true. Effort is the one thing that reliably blocks it — so hold it lightly, notice whatever is actually there, and let it be uninteresting. It is like falling asleep: you can arrange the conditions, but you can't force the moment.

      Say it in your own voice

      Let the phrase be yours, thought silently. The guided track only shows you the shape and the pacing; the practice itself is you thinking the words, in your head, at your own speed — which is why it fades to a timer and then to nothing. In the older books these phrases are called formulae.

      Short and often, not long and rare

      Ninety seconds, three times a day. Frequency builds the response; duration does not. One long session a day is not the same thing and will not do the same thing.

      What the notes mean

      Practising without a recording, the notes are the only thing that reaches you with your eyes shut.

      One note to begin. One more, partway, when the formula is done and the week's second exercise starts — only on the days that carry one. Two rising notes when it is time to take it back: arms, breath, eyes. Two low notes when you are done.

      What heaviness actually feels like

      Forty seconds that show you rather than tell you: clench your fist hard, let go, and notice what the forearm does for the twenty seconds afterwards. That release is heaviness. Worth repeating any time the word has gone abstract on you.

      If it feels wrong

      Nothing is happening

      Expect nothing for the first few days. It usually appears somewhere between a few days and two weeks, and may arrive as warmth, tingling, sinking, or the arm feeling larger or further away — those all count.

      Restlessness or emotion comes up

      That is a recognised part of the process — the old books call it an autogenic discharge — and not a sign that something is wrong. If it is uncomfortable: open your eyes, move your arms and legs, and stop there for that session.

      Background

      Why there are two methods here

      Autogenic training is very good at one thing: getting you into a settled state on demand, reliably, in ninety seconds. It is also strangely empty. All six of its exercises are ways in — heaviness, warmth, the heartbeat — and none of them is anything to do once you are there.

      Yoga nidra has the opposite problem. It is almost all content — a resolve held as already true, opposite sensations invited and released, resting as the awareness that was there the whole time — and it has no way of teaching any of it. It arrives as a recording you lie down and listen to, so nothing is ever retrieved, only recognised. The recording carries on whether or not you are still awake, which is why so many people finish one having slept through the middle and count it as a practice.

      So each supplies what the other lacks. Autogenic training lends its method: one element at a time, the same words every time, and nothing new until the last thing arrives on its own. Nidra lends somewhere to go once you can get there.

      That is why the nidra elements are attached to the end of a practice from the third step rather than being practised on their own. They need the state the formula has just produced. Doing them cold is a different and much harder exercise, and it is the one that does not stick.

      Where it comes from

      Worked out in Berlin in the 1920s, as an attempt to take the hypnotist out of hypnosis — to let people reach the same settled state on their own, with nobody guiding them.

      What people notice

      Less noise in the head — not silence, the volume down. Coming down faster after something stressful. Settling into sleep. A cleaner head between two demanding things. What arrives, and when, varies a lot between people.

      What this is

      Autogenic training, and yoga nidra.

      A way in, and somewhere to go. Two methods, each of which supplies what the other is missing. Nobody teaches them together, and separately each one is missing its other half.

      The way in

      A switch you can reach for.

      You repeat one short phrase to yourself — my right arm is heavy — and after a few days the arm actually does feel heavy. You notice it happen. Nothing to concentrate on and nothing to believe.

      Less noise in your head. Coming down faster after something stressful. Getting to sleep, instead of lying there running the day back.

      It is very good at getting you somewhere, and it has nothing at all for you to do once you arrive.

      Somewhere to go

      What the state is for.

      Three things, and each of them needs the state you have just made.

      You say one sentence about how you mean to be, phrased as though it were already so. You call up a feeling and then its opposite — heavy, then light — and let both go. And you spend a few minutes with nothing to do at all, which is harder than it sounds.

      On its own this is close to unlearnable, because it only ever comes as a recording to lie down and listen to, and a recording carries on whether or not you are still awake.
