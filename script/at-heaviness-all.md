# Heaviness — arms and legs

**Completes the first standard exercise · ~90 seconds**

The dominant arm was the training ground. This step is the classical formula
itself — *my arms and legs are heavy* — and it is where the response
generalises from the limb that learned it to the rest of the body.

Practise it yourself: ninety seconds, three times a day, no audio. This
recording is here so you know the shape, not to be played daily.

## Why one step and not three

Heaviness used to take four: right arm, left arm, both arms, legs. Four weeks,
and three of them spent proving something the body does on its own. The
response is *learned* once, in the dominant arm, against the resistance of
never having felt it before. After that it is not being learned again — it is
being named somewhere else, and it goes there mostly by itself.

So the arm keeps its own step, because that is the hard one and the only one
where nothing may happen for a fortnight. Everything after it is one formula:
the standard one, naming all four limbs, exactly as the older texts state it.

The recording opens on the arm you already have. That is not a second formula
creeping in — it is the anchor, said once, to start from something present
rather than something hoped for.

## Narration

<!-- narration:start -->

### Practice

One arm is yours now. The formula names all four limbs, and mostly they follow
the one that learned it.

[pause 2]

Settle. Eyes closed.

[pause 3]

Silently, in your own voice.

[pause 2]

I am completely calm.

[pause 3]

Start from the arm you have. You do not have to work for it.

[pause 3]

My right arm is heavy.

[pause 4]

My arms and legs are heavy.

[pause 5]

My arms and legs are heavy.

[pause 5]

My arms and legs are heavy.

[pause 5]

My arms and legs are heavy.

[pause 5]

I am completely calm.

[pause 4]

### Cancellation

Bend and stretch your arms. Fists clenched, firmly.

[pause 3]

A deep breath in — and out.

[pause 2]

And open your eyes.
