# Briefing — Warmth

**~70 seconds · offered when you reach it, before its first practice**

<!-- render: --speed 1.0 --lead-in 0.4 -->

Each new exercise introduces itself at the moment it starts, rather than being
explained weeks earlier in a single upfront lecture nobody can hold. This is the
"what does this one actually do" film for this step.

---

## Narration

<!-- narration:start -->

New exercise. Heaviness was muscle; this one is blood.

[pause 0.9]

The phrase is: my right arm is warm.

[pause 0.8]

What you are after is circulation — blood coming back into the hands and
forearms, which is one of the things your body does as soon as it stops
bracing.

[pause 0.9]

Do not picture a fire, or rub your hands together first. There is nothing to
imagine. Say the phrase, and notice the hands.

[pause 0.9]

For most people this one turns up faster than heaviness did, because you have
already learned the harder half — letting it happen instead of doing it.

[pause 0.9]

Cold hands at first are common, and are not a failure.

[pause 0.8]

Let the heaviness come back first, then add the warmth. Ninety seconds, three
times a day.

<!-- narration:end -->
