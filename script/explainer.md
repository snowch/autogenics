# Autogenic Training — Explainer

**How to actually do it · 4:12**

<!-- render: --speed 1.0 --stretch 1.0 --lead-in 0.5 -->

Listen to this once, before your first practice. It carries the teaching, so
that the practice tracks don't have to — a session that stops to explain itself
breaks the very state it is trying to produce.

**This is the *how*; the intro film is the *why*.** They used to overlap by
about half: both opened on what autogenic training is, both listed the six
exercises, both gave the dose, both closed on ending up not needing the app.
Two "consume this once before you start" assets is one too many, so the film
keeps the pitch and the arc, and this keeps the technique — why heaviness, how
to hold the phrase, what a discharge is, and how to finish. Nothing here is in
the film.

It also carried a stale section telling the listener to go and find "the one
called the example" and mentioning seven- and fourteen-minute sessions. That
described a folder of files, from before the app existed and chose the track
for you. It is cut.

## Production notes

This is the one track that is **not** relaxation audio. It is delivered at
normal conversational pace, with short pauses for structure rather than dwell
time. The render settings above are declared in this file so a later re-render
can't silently slow it to session pace.

- Voice: clear, direct, unhurried but not soft. Explaining, not soothing.
- Pauses of 1–2 seconds mark topic changes. Nothing longer.
- The narrator voice is deliberately slow, so this track runs at `--speed 1.0`
  while the practices sit lower. Information wants pace; induction does not.

## Narration

<!-- narration:start -->

First, briefly: this is a self-training method, not medical treatment, and
nothing here is medical advice. If something's wrong, see a doctor.

[pause 1.5]

With that said. You already know what this is for. This is how you actually do
it — about four minutes. You don't need to relax for this part, just listen
normally.

[pause 1.5]

The name is the first clue. Autogenic means generated from within. You are not
being relaxed by me, or by a recording. You are learning to produce the state
yourself.

[pause 1.5]

So, why heaviness. When your body shifts out of alert mode, your muscles
release the low background tension they hold all day. Heaviness is what that
release feels like from the inside. So the sensation isn't really the goal —
it's the signal that the shift has happened.

[pause 1.5]

What you'll actually do is repeat one short phrase: my right arm is heavy.

[pause 1]

Say it silently, inside your own head, in your own voice. Not aloud. Four to
six times, slowly, and then let it go.

[pause 1.5]

Now the part that catches everyone.

[pause 1]

You cannot make this happen by trying. Effort is the one thing that reliably
blocks it. If you concentrate hard on your arm and demand that it feel heavy,
it won't.

[pause 1]

It's like falling asleep. You can arrange the conditions, but you can't force
the moment. So hold the phrase lightly, notice whatever is actually there, and
let it be uninteresting. This is called passive concentration, and it's the
whole skill.

[pause 2]

And it may not arrive as heaviness exactly. People describe warmth, tingling,
sinking, or the arm feeling larger, or further away than it is. Any of those
count.

[pause 1.5]

Sometimes you'll get the opposite: restlessness, a twitch, an urge to move, or
unexpected emotion. That's known as an autogenic discharge. It's a recognised
part of the process, not a sign that something is wrong. If it's uncomfortable,
open your eyes, move your arms and legs, and stop there for that session.

[pause 2]

Every practice ends the same way, and this part is not optional.

[pause 1]

Bend and stretch your arms firmly, fists clenched, several times. Take two deep
breaths. Then open your eyes. This returns your muscle tone and circulation to
normal. Skip it and you can be left groggy or heavy-headed for a while
afterwards.

[pause 1]

The one exception is if you're practising in bed to fall asleep. In that case,
don't do it — just stay lying down and let yourself drift off.

[pause 2]

Two cautions before you start. Don't practise while driving, or doing anything
else that needs your attention. And deep relaxation can leave you a little
light-headed when you stand up — so if you have low blood pressure, a heart
condition, diabetes, or epilepsy, speak to your doctor before making this a
daily habit.

[pause 2]

Notice that everything I've described is something you do, not something a
recording does to you. The app guides your first few practices, then steps
back, and then stops — because you are the one producing this.

[pause 2]

That's everything. Go and do the first one.

[pause 1]

Little, and often.

<!-- narration:end -->
