# Segment — the close

**~15 seconds · structure · fires at the end of every session except an
explicit sleep practice**

<!-- render: --speed 0.90 --lead-in 0.4 -->

Carried over from the existing take-back, which was already ours and already
worked. It is structure: it runs whatever the fade tier, and it is chimed as
well as spoken, because it is the one part an eyes-closed user must not miss.

The fourth line is a briefing, not a cue — it plays for the first few sessions
and then stops. The three cues above it never change. Rescue never plays the
briefing: it is guided forever, and a practice you reach for in a state should
not spend its first seconds on explanation.

**Screen cues:** the three lines below, in order.

---

## The shrink ladder

| reps | what is said |
| --- | --- |
| under 8 | the briefing, then the three cues |
| 8–19 | the three cues |
| 20+ | **Take it back.** |

**The close is the one layer that never goes to nothing.** Every other layer
ends at silence and you run it yourself; this one keeps a line for ever, because
it is the only part of the practice with a caution attached and the onboarding
promises the app always runs it. One line is a cheap price for not quietly
dropping the thing that stops you standing up light-headed.

## Narration

<!-- narration:start -->

Take it back now.

[pause 0.5]

Bend and stretch your arms — fists clenched, firmly. Two or three times.

[pause 2.5]

A deep breath in. And out.

[pause 2.0]

And open your eyes.

<!-- narration:end -->

---

## Narration — first few sessions only

<!-- narration:start -->

That part is not optional, and it is not a wind-down. It puts the ordinary tone
back in the muscles before you stand up. Skipping it is what leaves people
heavy-headed for an hour afterwards.

<!-- narration:end -->
